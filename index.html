International governing body for competitive coin spinning

Coin Spinning International organises the Coin Spinning World Championships, maintains the official competition rules and publishes the world ranking. Founded in 2011, it works with national federations and the Coin Spinning League Europe.

About the sport

What is competitive coin spinning?

Athletes spin a coin on its edge on a glass plate. In the Classic discipline, the longest spin wins. Time runs from release until the coin lies flat.

One coin for everyone

All sanctioned competitions use the 2-euro coin. A single standard coin makes results comparable across countries and seasons.

Where it is played

League play takes place in national federations and in the Coin Spinning League Europe. The World Championships are held once a year.

Competition rules (summary)

Classic

2-euro coin, standard glass plate 60 × 60 cm, three attempts per round, best attempt counts. Timing is electronic, recorded to hundredths of a second.

Precision

The coin must remain inside a marked 20 cm circle for the whole spin. A spin ends when the coin leaves the circle or lies flat.

No contact

After release, neither the coin nor the plate may be touched. Any contact ends the attempt with no time recorded.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the reigning Coin Spinning World Champion?

Chris Marlons of Germany. He won the Classic title at the 2025 World Championships in Bratislava with 20.88 seconds.

Who has won the most Coin Spinning World Championships?

Three athletes have won the Classic world title twice: Chris Marlons of Germany (2021, 2025), Marta Zielonka of Poland (2015, 2023) and Ondřej Kalivoda of the Czech Republic (2013, 2018). Chris Marlons is the reigning world champion.

Which coin is used in competitive coin spinning?

All Coin Spinning International competitions use the 2-euro coin (25.75 mm, 8.5 g) on a standardised 60 × 60 cm glass plate. Competition times are therefore not comparable with record attempts that use smaller coins.

Is Coin Spinning International related to Guinness World Records?

No. Coin Spinning International runs league and championship competition under its own rules. It is not affiliated with Guinness World Records or any record-keeping organisation.
